## Script
**[OPEN]**
Good morning. This is The Southern Africa Brief for Friday, the 27th of November. Today: a new court built around survivors of sexual violence, a media skills academy for young people, and keeping children safe online.

**[STORY 1]**
Khayelitsha in the Western Cape now has a dedicated Sexual Offences Court. It was opened on Wednesday, the first day of the 16 Days of Activism, by Minister Sindisiwe Chikunga and Deputy Minister of Justice Andries Nel. The court offers 10 specialised support services. These include court preparation officers, intermediaries, separate waiting areas, information in Braille and large print, and rooms where survivors can testify by closed-circuit camera, away from the accused. Vulnerable witnesses may also testify virtually or use sign language. It sits alongside the Khayelitsha Thuthuzela Care Centre, which is open 24 hours a day. That centre handled 428 cases in the first half of this financial year.

Why it matters: many survivors never report, or drop their cases, because court is frightening. The National Prosecuting Authority says the conviction rate in sexual offences trials was 74 percent in the 2025 to 2026 financial year, and 79.2 percent for cases that went through Thuthuzela Care Centres. Support from the start makes a difference.

What to do: if you work with women, girls or young people, find out where your nearest Thuthuzela Care Centre is, and share the Gender-Based Violence Command Centre number. It is free and open day and night: oh eight hundred, four two eight, four two eight.

**[STORY 2]**
President Cyril Ramaphosa is due today to launch the CANAL+ Academy at MultiChoice City in Johannesburg. The academy brings together the training programmes of CANAL+ and the MultiChoice Group, including the MultiChoice Talent Factory. It focuses on young people, women and emerging talent. It will cover audiovisual production, broadcasting, journalism, content creation and technical operations. In its first year it expects to train between 800 and 1,000 participants.

Why it matters: the creative and media industries need technical skills that schools rarely teach. A structured route into film, television and digital content can turn a young person's interest into a career. No application details have been published yet.

What to do: if you know a young person who makes videos, writes or wants to work behind the camera, help them build a simple portfolio now. When applications open, they will be ready. Namela keeps a free list of verified bursaries, learnerships and funding at opportunities dot namela dot org.

**[STORY 3]**
A government article published on Tuesday, following World Children's Day, argues that children's rights and safety must extend into the digital world. It draws on the South Africa Kids Online study by UNICEF South Africa and the Department of Social Development, from 2020. In that study, 70 percent of children surveyed used the internet without their parents' consent. A quarter had added people they had never met face to face as online friends or contacts. 18 percent had sent a photo or video of themselves to someone they had never met in person. The article points to existing protections, including the Cybercrimes Act and the Film and Publication Board. The Department of Basic Education also has an online safety guideline in the Life Orientation curriculum.

Why it matters: more children now have cheaper smartphones. Access brings learning, but also risk. Online abuse is part of the violence the 16 Days campaign is about.

What to do: parents and caregivers, talk to your children about who they chat to online, and agree simple household rules. Teachers, use the online safety lessons in Life Orientation before the holidays begin, when children spend more time on their phones.

**[NUMBER TO REMEMBER]**
74 percent. That is the conviction rate in sexual offences trials last financial year. When survivors are supported to see a case through, justice is far more likely.
