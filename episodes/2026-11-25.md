## Script
**[OPEN]**
Good morning. This is The Southern Africa Brief for Wednesday, the 25th of November.
Today: the 16 Days of Activism begins, matric exams reach their final day, and a new report on why mobile internet coverage is not the same as use.

**[STORY 1]**
The 16 Days of Activism for No Violence Against Women and Children starts today. It runs until the 10th of December. This year's national theme is "Letsema: Men, Women, Boys and Girls working together to end Gender-Based Violence and Femicide". The national launch took place last Friday in Middelburg, Mpumalanga. It included the opening of a new Thuthuzela Care Centre at King Nyabela Hospital.
Why it matters: the theme puts the focus on everyone, not only on women. Boys and men are named as part of the solution. The campaign also overlaps with World AIDS Day on the 1st of December and the International Day of Persons with Disabilities on the 3rd of December.
What to do: teachers and youth workers can use these sixteen days to run one honest conversation with boys about respect and consent. And everyone can save one number. The 24-hour Gender-Based Violence Command Centre is on zero eight zero zero, four two eight, four two eight. You can also SMS the word HELP to three one five three one.

**[STORY 2]**
The 2026 National Senior Certificate exams end tomorrow, the 26th of November. The Department of Basic Education says 962 thousand, 832 candidates registered this year. More than 815 thousand of them are full-time learners. The Minister has called it the largest full-time group ever to write. Marking centres open between the 26th and the 28th of November. Marking itself starts on the 1st of December, with more than 51 thousand teachers recruited as markers.
Why it matters: the end of exams is a risky time for young people. The department has strongly discouraged so-called pens-down parties, pointing to alcohol and substance abuse. It has asked parents, school governing bodies and communities to give guidance and supervision.
What to do: parents, know where your matriculant is going this weekend and who they will be with. Young people, celebrate, but do it safely. And use the weeks before results to plan. Look at bursaries, learnerships and second-chance options now, not in January. Namela keeps a free list of verified bursaries, learnerships and funding at opportunities dot namela dot org.

**[STORY 3]**
The GSMA, the global body for mobile operators, launched its Digital Africa Index 2026 in Cape Town last Wednesday. It found that mobile broadband now reaches 92 percent of Africa's population. But only 28 percent use mobile internet on their own devices. That leaves a usage gap of 64 percent. South Africa scored highest on the index's measure of digital development, with 69 out of 100. The report also looked at South Africa's removal of the 9 percent excise duty on smartphones costing under 2,500 rand, in April 2025. According to GSMA analysis, monthly sales of entry-level smartphones rose from around 250 thousand in March 2025 to around 460 thousand in February 2026.
Why it matters: the signal is already there for most people. What holds them back is the cost of a device, the cost of data and the skills to use it. Cheaper phones are one lever that clearly moved.
What to do: community organisations and libraries can help people take the next step. Run a short session on using a phone for job searches, applications and government services. If you run a youth programme, ask your learners whether they have their own smartphone. The answer may change how you share information.

**[NUMBER TO REMEMBER]**
64 percent. That is the share of Africans who live within reach of mobile broadband but do not use mobile internet. Coverage is no longer the main problem. Access to devices, data and skills is.
